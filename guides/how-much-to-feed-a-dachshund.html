How much should you feed a dachshund? Work it out in calories, not cups

how much should I feed my dachshund

Updated 2026-09-12 · Every figure sourced · Editorial policy

Short answerStart from calories, not cups. Resting energy requirement is 70 × (body weight in kg)^0.75, and a neutered adult multiplies that by 1.6. An 11-pound dachshund lands near 375 kcal a day, a 16-pound dog near 482, and a 25-pound dog near 678. Individual needs vary by up to 50%, so adjust to body condition.

How much should you feed a dachshund?

Every honest answer starts with a calorie figure and ends with your hands on the dog. The formula gives you a starting point. The dog tells you whether the starting point was right. Anyone who gives you a cup number without asking his weight, his neuter status and the calorie density of your food is guessing.

How do you work out the number yourself?

Two steps, and the arithmetic is the same one your veterinarian uses. Resting energy requirement is 70 × (body weight in kg)0.75. Then multiply by a maintenance factor for the dog in front of you. Both come from the Pet Nutrition Alliance.

The table is a starting point, and the variation is enormous

The Pet Nutrition Alliance states that individual variation runs up to ±50%. Two neutered 16-pound dachshunds in the same house can genuinely need 240 and 720 calories. Adjust to body condition, not to the table.

How do you turn calories into cups?

Find the kcal per cup on your bag — it is on the label, usually near the feeding chart, sometimes stated per kilogram as well. Divide the daily calorie figure by it. Then split the result across the day’s meals.

The arithmetic for a 16-pound neutered adult at 482 kcal: a food listing 400 kcal per cup gives about 1.2 cups a day; a food listing 300 kcal per cup gives about 1.6. Those two numbers are arithmetic, not a claim about any particular product — calorie density varies widely between foods, and it is the reason a cup measurement transferred from one bag to another can quietly overfeed a dog by a third.

  • Measure with a kitchen scale in grams if you can. A "cup" scooped from a bag varies by how you scoop it.
  • Re-do the arithmetic every time you change food. Every time.
  • Subtract treats from the total before you portion the meals, not after.

Why does the chart on the bag overfeed?

It is not a conspiracy, it is a category error. The bag’s chart is a broad population range printed for every dog of a given weight — intact and neutered, working and sedentary, adolescent and elderly. It cannot know that your dog is a neutered five-year-old who walks forty minutes a day. Given a range and no other information, most owners read the top of it, and the difference between the top and the bottom of a population range in a breed with a 24.4% lifetime disc disease risk is not a rounding error.

How many treats can a dachshund have?

48 kcaldaily treat allowance for a 16 lb dachshund
Tufts Petfoodology, 10% rule

Treats should account for no more than 10% of daily calories. For that 16-pound dog on 482 kcal, the allowance is roughly 48 calories a day — which is less than most owners picture. A dachshund being trained through a session can burn through it in ten minutes, which is an argument for training with part of his measured dinner rather than with anything extra.

The other 90% has to be complete and balanced, which under FDA labeling rules means the food meets an AAFCO nutrient profile or has passed an AAFCO feeding trial. Note that there is no such thing as "AAFCO approved" — AAFCO has no regulatory authority, and state feed control officials do the enforcing.

How do you know the number is right?

You score his body condition, weekly, with your hands. The 1-to-9 scale is the standard, and the target is 4 to 5. A dog at 7 out of 9 is carrying roughly 30% body fat.

  1. Ribs. Run flat palms along his sides. You should feel each rib easily under a thin layer, with light pressure. If you have to press, he is over. If they are sharp and visible from across the room, he is under.
  2. From above. Look down. There should be a visible waist behind the ribs. On a long dog this reads as a narrowing rather than an hourglass — do not expect a whippet.
  3. From the side. The belly should tuck up from the ribcage toward the hind legs, not run parallel to the floor.
  4. Weigh weekly, same scale, same time of day. Change the calorie figure by about 10% and hold it for two to three weeks before judging.

Does keeping him lean protect his back?

Not on the evidence we have, and this is where most dachshund feeding advice quietly overclaims. DachsLife 2015 found no significant association between body condition score and intervertebral disc disease in 2,031 dogs. The link everyone assumes exists has not been demonstrated in this breed.

Keep him lean anyway, for a reason that is better supported. In Purina’s lifetime study of 48 Labradors, restricting calories by 25% added about 1.8 years of median lifespan. That is a large effect for something that costs nothing. It is a longevity argument and a joint argument, not a spine argument, and the difference is worth holding onto — because an owner who believes thinness is back insurance may skip the things that do have evidence behind them.

What about puppies and weight loss?

A growing puppy uses a factor of 2 to 3 times RER, which is why a 10-week-old eats an amount that looks absurd next to an adult’s bowl, and why that amount must come down as growth slows rather than becoming the permanent ration. A dog on a formal weight-loss plan uses a factor of 1.0 — RER alone — and that plan belongs with your veterinarian, who should be weighing him and checking that the drop is fat rather than muscle.

If he is already heavy, do not simply cut the current amount by eye. Work out RER for his target weight, feed to that, and re-score every two weeks. Use the weight guide to decide what the target should be, because the AKC divisions and the body condition scale answer different questions.

Questions people also ask

How many cups should a dachshund eat per day?

There is no universal cup figure, because calorie density differs between foods. Work out the calories first — about 375 a day for an 11-pound neutered adult, 482 at 16 pounds, 678 at 25 pounds — then divide by the kcal per cup printed on your bag, and re-do the arithmetic whenever you change food.

How often should you feed a dachshund?

Twice a day suits most adult dachshunds, and it splits the ration into portions that feel like meals. Puppies need three or four smaller meals. Note that twice-daily feeding is good practice for other reasons, not bloat prevention: gastric dilatation and volvulus is a disease of deep, narrow chests, and dachshunds are not a listed at-risk breed.

Why is my dachshund always hungry?

Appetite is a poor guide to requirement in this breed. Work from the calorie figure and body condition score instead. If he is scoring 4 to 5 out of 9 and holding weight, he is being fed correctly regardless of how he behaves at the bowl. A sudden genuine change in appetite is worth a veterinary conversation.

How do I know if my dachshund is overweight?

Use the 1-to-9 body condition scale and aim for 4 to 5. You should feel the ribs easily with light pressure, see a waist from above, and see the belly tuck up from the side. A dog scoring 7 out of 9 is carrying roughly 30% body fat. Weigh weekly on the same scale.

Does being overweight cause IVDD in dachshunds?

DachsLife 2015, surveying 2,031 owners, found no significant association between body condition score and disc disease. That is a British dataset and an absence of evidence rather than proof of no effect. Keep him lean for lifespan — Purina’s Labrador study found 25% calorie restriction added about 1.8 years of median life.

How many treats can I give a dachshund while training?

Keep all treats under 10% of daily calories — around 48 kcal for a 16-pound dog, which disappears fast in a training session. The practical fix is to train with part of his measured daily ration rather than with extra food, so a heavy training week does not become a heavy dog.
